Club Bar · Terminal · UX concept · September 2026

Stammplatz

The buying loop is fast, and since last week it moves. The next jump is not more motion. It is recognition: a terminal that behaves like the bartender who already knows your order, your Deckel, and the room.

Three moves, one theme. Each ships on its own, none touches the server or the schema, and every one of them respects the rules the terminal already lives by: zero idle frames, nothing personal on a public screen that was not already there, and one owner for the session.

Stammplatz
your regular spot at the bar

A first category that is yours: what you actually buy here, plus last time's round as one tile.

Der Deckel
the tab as a beer mat

The receipt says Prost, Jana! and pencils tonight's strokes onto a Bierdeckel, one per item: the round is on the Deckel, in the picture every member already has of it.

Das Brett
the idle screen as the club's board

The club's name, the scan invitation, and what the bar poured today. Repainted on events only, never on a clock.

Move 1 · after the scan

Stammplatz: the first tap is the drink you came for

When a returning member scans in, the first category chip is theirs. Its tiles are the products this member actually buys at this terminal, ranked by recency-weighted count over the terminal's own booking history, as many as fit one grid page. Ahead of them sits one tile that is not a product: Nochmal die Runde, last visit's cart in one tap.

Taps after the scan, ending on Bezahlen. The single beer does not get faster; it gets found. The round is where the time goes today, and where it comes back.

The data is already on the device

transactions_local keeps every synced booking with its product, member and session id, and nothing purges it. Ranking is one query. Nothing new syncs, nothing leaves the terminal, no migration, no server work.

First visit

No history means no Stammplatz. The chip reads Beliebt and holds the terminal's house favourites across everyone, with one line under the member bar: Tippe ein Getränk, dann Bezahlen. The second visit is personal.

Frozen catalogue, respected

The usuals are resolved against the session snapshot (ADR-0027 rule 11). A product deactivated since last visit is simply not there; a price change shows the new price, because the tile is the tile.

Erased members never rank

An anonymised member's row is tombstoned, so their card scans as unknown and their history is never read. The house favourites count bookings, not people, and a tombstoned member's rows are excluded from those too.

Two terminals, two memories

History is this terminal's. The Küche terminal learns your food, the Ruderbar your beer. That is right, not a gap, and the section on data locality below says what a second terminal, a wiped one, and an offline one each see.

Who can see it

The screen is read by whoever stands at the bar. A row of your usuals says less than the Deckel and the Buchungen button already on the same screen, and it names no number, no date, no age.

Move 2 · after the purchase

Der Deckel: the receipt is a beer mat, and it says your name

The app already calls the tab a Deckel, and German has the idiom ready: das kommt auf den Deckel, einen Strich machen. So the receipt is a beer mat: a coaster in the club's colours with a printed tally motif, and the moment the booking lands a pencil draws one stroke per item in tonight's Warenkorb onto it, one after another. Most members buy one thing after training, so most nights that is one stroke; a round for the table is a gate of five, closed with the diagonal. It reflects only tonight, which the receipt already knows, and claims nothing about the month. The receipt says Prost, Jana! instead of Buchung erfolgreich!, then the lines, the total, and the tab as it now stands, exactly as today.

Tonight only, and that is the point

The strokes are the items on this receipt, summed over quantities, which the receipt already holds. They say nothing about the month: the printed gates are obviously print, and the euro figure beside them is the only figure for the tab. So the mat is true about the one thing it draws and silent about everything else, and needs no data the terminal does not have.

Gates of five

Four uprights and a diagonal, the way a Deckel is kept. A second gate starts beside the first. The pencil stops at fifteen strokes, three gates, which covers any round a person carries from a bar; a cart of thirty sauna tokens is a dispenser sale, not an evening, and three closed gates say "a lot" well enough.

Cheap, and bounded

Stroke-offset animations on a handful of paths, paths and transforms only, staggered so five strokes finish inside 1.2 s, skipped under reduced motion. The same rules #921 set for the buying loop. Pure Flutter, no data, no sync.

The member bar, a little

The same coaster, small, beside the balance, with its printed motif and no pencil. It ties the word on the bar to the object on the receipt, so Dein Deckel jetzt reads as a thing the member has, not a caption.

If the mat should ever show the month

The count of drinks on the open tab is one map away: the server could return it beside member_balances in the same response, cached with the balance, exact and offline-safe. Not proposed, because tonight's strokes need nothing. Noted so the door is known to be there.

The greeting, too

The login burst says Hi Jana! at nine in the morning and at eleven at night. It should say Guten Morgen, Jana, Hallo, Jana, Guten Abend, Jana. Three strings per language and the terminal's own clock.

Why it is professional

A member walking up sees a bar with an evening in it and their club's name on it, instead of a spinner-shaped disc and a build string. The disc and Durstig? stay exactly where they are; they are the job.

Why it is cheap

One aggregate query over transactions_local for today, a rebuild on two existing events, a floor. The version string moves to the corner; the status modal already reports it.

Data locality · offline · several terminals

What each terminal knows, and what it may pretend to

The terminal is offline-first (ADR-0033) and there are two of them in service, in different rooms. Every screen here renders from the local cache alone, and a card scan never waits on the network. The rule for what to derive where is the one ADR-0042 and ADR-0047 already apply: derive on the device what must be right offline (the tab, the limit, the age), and let the server supply, cached, only what goes stale harmlessly. A suggestion is harmless when stale. A balance is not.

Level 0 ships first, and it is complete

Everything above needs no server, no schema change and no new sync step. The two terminals disagree about a member's usuals only in the way two bartenders in two rooms do, and the tab, which is the thing that must agree, already does: the balance comes from the server on every members delta and the local outbox is added on top (UC-T14).

Level 1, if the club wants one memory

The server already holds every booking from every terminal, so it can compute what no single terminal can: a member's usuals across rooms, their last round wherever it was bought, and the club's evening. Three protocol extensions in the next section carry that to the terminals, cached, on the same cursor pattern the contract already uses. Offline, a terminal shows the last answer it got, which is exactly as good as a suggestion needs to be.

Merging the two

With Level 1 the terminal takes the server's list and layers its own rows that are newer than the list's computed_at on top, so the beer bought thirty seconds ago counts before the next sync, and counts offline. The last round is the server's when it is newer, the terminal's otherwise.

The board, club-wide

Same shape: the server's day, with this terminal's unsynced rows added, and the label switches from an dieser Theke to im Verein only when a server board is present and dated today. Worth having when the terminals share a room; harmless when they do not.

What locality does not change

Erasure still rides the ordinary delta: a tombstoned member's rows are excluded from every query here, on every terminal, within one sync interval. No terminal gains any member data it does not hold today. Level 1 would add a list of product ids per member, nothing more.

Timing at the scan

The scan-to-grid budget is 500 ms and the products screen must not wait for a ranking. The query runs off the session start and the chip appears when it returns, which at this scale is the same frame. It is never fetched.

Protocol extensions · Level 1 · ADR-0033 amendment

Three additions to the terminal API, shaped like the contract already is

The sync contract has two shapes: a delta stream with a cursor (/sync/members, /sync/products) and a whole document re-read each cycle (/sync/config). The additions use the same two, and the same rule for choosing between them. One field joins the upload. Nothing is fetched at scan time; everything lands in the cache on the ordinary 60-second cycle and is read from there.

POST /sync/transactions

A. session_id on the uploaded transaction

One optional field on Transaction, owned by the terminal like id is. It says "these rows were one order at the bar", which the terminal has known all along (transactions_local.session_id) and never told anyone. The server stores it as sent and never interprets it beyond grouping. The allowlist admits it; additionalProperties: false lists it; the migration adds one nullable column, and the master ERM says why.

This is what lets the server know a round at all. Without it, extension B can rank usuals but cannot say what last time's order was, and staff reading the ledger cannot see that five lines were one purchase.

B. A delta stream of each member's usuals

Why not on the member row. The contract already argues this twice, for the ceiling and for the band: /sync/members is a delta on updated_at, and a purchase touches no member row. Usuals change on purchase. Riding the member delta would mean bumping updated_at on every beer, which re-sends the whole member row, birth date included, for a change to a suggestion. So usuals get their own stream with their own key, exactly as categories and products do.

What one entry says. Up to ten product ids with a weight, and the last round as product and quantity pairs. No amounts, no timestamps beyond the round's, no names. The weight is a recency-weighted count with a 30-day half-life over the last 90 days, written down once in the ADR so the on-device Level 0 ranking and the server's agree; the server additionally leaves out purchases that were stornoed, which the terminal cannot know.

Erasure rides the same cursor. An anonymised member is emitted once more with empty products, a null last_round and a fresh computed_at, the way the birth date arrives nulled. The terminal's cache forgets them within one sync interval, on every terminal.

GET /sync/board

C. The club's day, as one whole document

No cursor: the day's counts per product across every terminal, top four, re-read whole each cycle like /sync/config. It is not folded into /sync/config, although that document invites the next club setting to join it, because a board is not a setting: it changes every minute and its cache lifetime is a day, not "until the treasurer edits it". Two documents with two lifetimes stay two endpoints.

The floor lives on the server, so every terminal keeps the same silence: below a handful of bookings pours is empty and the terminals show nothing. The day is the club's calendar day (pattern 020), and a terminal discards a cached board whose day is not its own today, so a Monday morning never opens with Saturday's evening on it. The terminal adds its unsynced rows on top, which is what keeps an offline terminal counting.

Considered and not taken

Returning usuals inside TransactionBatchResponse next to member_balances: tempting, since the terminal already names members there, but it only ever covers members this terminal has seen, which is precisely the case Level 1 exists to fix. The delta stream covers a member before their first scan on a new terminal. A count of drinks on the open tab in that same response would make the mat's strokes real; it is not proposed, because the mat is decoration by decision.

Enforcement, as the contract has it

Both new response schemas and the widened Transaction carry additionalProperties: false; the OAS enforces in CI and the server allowlist guards production (ADR-0033 §8). API tests: the cursor semantics of B, the erased-member entry, the ten-id cap, a stornoed purchase not counting; the floor and the midnight boundary of C in the club's timezone; A round-tripping to the ledger.

What the terminals gain in data

Per member, a list of product ids and one round. That is the whole widening, and it is data the terminal that booked it already holds. It is still habit data, so it gets the same treatment the birth date got: named in the ERM, erased through the ordinary delta, never rendered beyond the tiles themselves.

Rollout

Backend first, terminals follow by OTA (ADR-0054). A terminal that does not yet call B or C is simply on Level 0; a terminal that sends A to a backend that ignores it loses nothing. There is no flag day.

Deliberately not

Personal touches that would be the wrong kind

Each of these came up while sketching, and each one runs into a rule the project already wrote down for a reason.

  • ×No birthday greeting. ADR-0045 rule 6 says the terminal never states a member's birth date, and Alles Gute zum Geburtstag states it to the whole room.
  • ×No streaks, badges or "your hundredth Helles". That is gamifying alcohol at a club whose membership includes people under 18, and whose terminal enforces Jugendschutz. Recognition, yes; reward, no.
  • ×No second card to split a round. ADR-0027 rule 3: a foreign card during an active session is rejected, and the reasons it gives still hold.
  • ×No ambient motion on the idle screen. #760 measured what a breathing disc costs a Pi. The board is still, and changes only when the bar does.
  • ×No server work in the first cut. Everything here is on-device and per terminal. Cross-terminal usuals are Level 1 above, a real later feature and a small one, once the on-device version has shown the shape is right.
  • ×No mat that pretends to know the month. The pencil draws tonight's items and nothing else; the gates above are print, and the tab is the euro figure. A stroke count the receipt cannot vouch for would be read as a billing error at a bar.
  • ×No network at scan time, ever. Nothing in these three moves fetches. A terminal that has been offline for a week shows the same Stammplatz, the same Deckel and the same board it would show online, from the same rows.
